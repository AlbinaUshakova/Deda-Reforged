# Hidden Course Sanity Audit

Date: 2026-09-04

## Scope

This pass is a light sanity-check for the currently hidden courses:

- Turkish (`tr`)
- Spanish (`es`)
- German (`de`)
- French (`fr`)
- Italian (`it`)
- English (`en`)

Reviewed sample:

- `ep1`
- `ep2` or the next early main-path lesson where relevant

## Overall finding

No immediate structural breakage stands out in the hidden-course sample.
The larger issue is product fit and lesson tone, not technical integrity.

## Course notes

### Turkish (`tr`)

- Structure is valid.
- The course already reads like a word-decoding path, but the opening lesson is uneven in reward.
- `anne`, `nane`, `nar` are concrete enough.
- `annene`, `anneanne`, and some call/command combinations feel more morphological than rewarding for such an early step.

Recommendation:

- Keep hidden from the main flow.
- Do not spend time polishing deeply unless Turkish becomes strategically important again.

### Spanish (`es`)

- Structure is valid.
- The opening lesson works technically, but it is too dominated by function words and grammar-like forms for the current product promise.
- `oso`, `rosa`, `oro` are the clearest early wins.
- `eres`, `es`, `a eso`, `sea`, `esa`, `ese` make the lesson feel more abstract than it should.

Recommendation:

- Keep hidden from the main flow.
- If Spanish is ever revived, rewrite the first lesson around more concrete nouns much earlier.

### German (`de`)

- Structure is valid.
- The course has real readable words early, but it mixes them with grammar/function-heavy cards too soon.
- `Tee`, `See`, `Eis`, `Nest`, `Milch`, `Tisch`, `Gans`, `Maus` are strong anchors.
- `ist`, `ein`, `eins`, `sein`, `ich`, `mich`, `dich` pull the early path toward grammar drills.

Recommendation:

- Keep hidden from the main flow.
- If reactivated later, tighten the opening lessons around object words before pronoun/case patterns.

### French (`fr`)

- Structure is valid.
- The first lesson is heavily article/pronoun-based.
- `lit`, `sel`, `rat`, `aile`, `mer`, `mur`, `lune` give usable concrete anchors.
- `le`, `la`, `il`, `elle` lead too strongly with grammar markers for the current product direction.

Recommendation:

- Keep hidden from the main flow.
- If revived, reduce article-led opening cards and move toward concrete reading wins faster.

### Italian (`it`)

- The cleanest hidden-course opening among the Latin-script tracks.
- `mamma`, `mela`, `mano`, `miele`, `limone` give clear readable wins early.
- Product fit is still weak for the unfamiliar-script positioning, but the lesson tone itself is solid.

Recommendation:

- Leave as is for now.
- If any hidden course deserves later polish first, Italian is the easiest candidate.

### English (`en`)

- Structure is valid, but product fit is weakest.
- The path behaves more like phonics-adjacent decoding than unfamiliar-script reading.
- Some object words exist (`tea`, `toast`, `tree`, `train`, `store`, `sister`), but the course direction is fundamentally different from the current Deda promise.

Recommendation:

- Keep hidden from the main flow.
- Do not polish further inside the current roadmap unless the product intentionally expands beyond unfamiliar scripts.

## Conclusion

- Hidden courses do not currently need urgent content surgery.
- Their main gap is strategic fit, not lesson integrity.
- The best use of effort is still:
  - keep Georgian as the benchmark,
  - keep Serbian as the secondary active course,
  - treat future core expansion as new implementation work for Korean, Russian, Japanese, and Arabic.
